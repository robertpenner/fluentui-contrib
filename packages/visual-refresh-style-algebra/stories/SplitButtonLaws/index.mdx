A SplitButton is two buttons pretending to be one. Its correctness is therefore mostly a claim about a **seam**: the joined edge is flat, the outer corners keep the family's radius, a visible boundary separates the actions, and both actions keep every guarantee a plain Button gives. And because the seam has a side, all of it flips with the writing direction.

The [Button laws](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-cap-button-laws--docs) page explains the observation model.

## What the laws say

**Inherited.** Both actions are native `<button type="button">`. Both draw a focus indicator, in ordinary and forced colors. Both suppress hover and pressed feedback when disabled.

**Propagation.** Disabling the SplitButton disables _both_ actions; making it disabled-focusable leaves _both_ reachable and both marked `aria-disabled`. Losing the menu button silently is worse than losing it visibly. Both actions also present the same background, text colour, and outer boundary for a given state.

**The seam.** The joined corners are squared and the outer corners are not, in LTR and in RTL. A divider colour is declared on the primary action's `::after` in every appearance, condition, and colour mode, and it is never `transparent`.

## Two things the laws had to be taught

**A pseudo-element is a separate box.** The divider lives on `::after`; folding its declarations into the primary action's own surface made the button look as though it painted a right border it does not have. Surfaces are now selected per pseudo-element.

**An ancestor is part of the claim.** Griffel keys its direction-specific SplitButton rules on a _static_ class and distinguishes them only by an ancestor:

```css
.f14uur2j .fui-SplitButton__primaryActionButton {
  border-top-right-radius: 0;
}
.fc1btbj .fui-SplitButton__primaryActionButton {
  border-top-left-radius: 0;
}
```

Matching on the key compound alone attributed both the LTR and the RTL variant to the same element — which reported _every_ corner as squared, and would have hidden a genuine RTL regression behind a passing test. The observer now verifies the ancestor before attributing the rule.

Both were defects in the instrument, not the product; they are worth recording because a silently over-permissive observer is the failure mode that makes a law suite worthless.

## The result

No CAP SplitButton defect was found. Every law holds in both writing directions across all six appearances and all three states. The seam is read out of the live CSSOM below, so the claim is checkable rather than asserted.

## What this page cannot tell you

Whether the two actions are the same height, whether the divider is actually visible against the surface behind it, and whether the outer corners look right at a given radius are paint and layout questions. They are left to the cross-engine checks.
