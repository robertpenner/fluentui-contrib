## The simple idea

A button case is a tuple of eleven choices: product, visual language, density, appearance, interaction state, color mode, content kind, icon placement, anatomy policy, composition, and direction.

If every value is paired with every other value, the result is the **raw Cartesian space**. Some pairs do not describe a supported button. The **valid space** keeps only tuples accepted by the explicit predicate.

The distinction is about this clean-room model's contract. A rejected tuple is outside the modeled domain; it is not a claim that no product could ever support it.

## Follow the four steps

1. Multiply the cardinalities of all eleven axes to get 138,240 raw tuples.
2. See why only four of the twelve content and icon-placement pairs are coherent.
3. Count supported appearance and anatomy combinations within each product and visual language, producing 29 rather than 48.
4. Recombine the independent groups: `240 × 4 × 29 = 27,840` valid cases.

## Why this matters for testing

Only about 20.1% of the raw tuples are valid. A generator that chose all eleven axes independently would therefore spend roughly four fifths of its samples producing inputs the resolver rejects. The package instead generates within the valid relationships, so property runs spend their effort challenging supported behavior.

The visualization and the exhaustive metric use the same exported domain declarations. `researchMetrics.test.ts` still enumerates every one of the 138,240 tuples and checks that exactly 27,840 satisfy the real validity predicate.
