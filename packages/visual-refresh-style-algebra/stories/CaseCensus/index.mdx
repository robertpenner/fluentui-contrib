<Title>Synthetic research: case census</Title>

This synthetic research page follows the production-first [CAP Button audit](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-cap-button-audit--docs) and [content anatomy audit](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-content-anatomy-audit--docs). The audit found four recurring content patterns, three constructible empty-button inputs outside the research model, and no product-support contract for the twelve inputs. With those limits visible, this page asks a narrower question: how does the historical model turn 138,240 synthetic tuples into 27,840 cases that its research resolvers promise to handle?

## The simple idea

A button case records eleven choices: product, visual language, density, appearance, interaction state, color mode, content kind, icon placement, anatomy policy, composition, and direction.

Pairing every value with every other value gives **138,240 review candidates**. The research model then applies three stated assumptions and keeps **27,840 model-admitted cases** for generation and comparison.

This narrowing makes generated tests spend their time on cases the resolvers promise to handle. It does not classify support on CAP's behalf. A case omitted by the model may still pass through production code, and a constructible case is not automatically promised by a product. The resulting 27,840 is a model-derived count, not a production CAP inventory.

## Follow the four steps

1. Multiply the cardinalities of all eleven axes to get 138,240 raw tuples.
2. Represent the audit's four recurring content patterns as four model cases, while documenting the eight pairings the model does not generate.
3. Count the assumed appearance and anatomy domains within each product and visual language, producing 29 rather than 48.
4. Recombine the independent groups: `240 × 4 × 29 = 27,840` model-admitted cases.

## Why this matters for testing

Only about 20.1% of the raw tuples satisfy the model predicate. A generator that chose all eleven axes independently would therefore spend roughly four fifths of its samples producing inputs the clean-room resolver rejects. The package instead generates within those declared relationships, so property runs spend their effort challenging the resolver's promised domain.

The visualization and the exhaustive metric use the same exported domain declarations. `researchMetrics.test.ts` still enumerates every one of the 138,240 tuples and checks that exactly 27,840 satisfy the model predicate.
