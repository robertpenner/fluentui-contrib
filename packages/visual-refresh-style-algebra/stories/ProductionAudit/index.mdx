This is the report of the CAP Button-family audit: what was examined, what was
found, what was changed, and where the evidence lives.

## The claim

CAP re-skins the Fluent Button family. The question this investigation asked was
not "does it look right" but "which of Fluent's guarantees survive the re-skin?"
That question has an answer only if the guarantees are written as laws over
observable style, and the laws are run against the production theme rather than
a reconstruction of it. Every finding below comes from a law failing against the
real `@fluentui-contrib/react-cap-theme` styles.

## The four investigations

Each one is a page you can open, read, and re-run:

- **[Griffel shorthand diagnostics](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-griffel-shorthand-diagnostics--docs)**
  — which declarations the build drops on the floor, and why the drop is silent
  in practice.
- **[CAP Button laws](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-cap-button-laws--docs)**
  — what an appearance is allowed to change, what a state is allowed to change,
  and what focus must always do.
- **[CAP ToggleButton laws](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-cap-togglebutton-laws--docs)**
  — which Button guarantees a ToggleButton inherits, which it specializes, and
  the one it contradicts.
- **[CAP SplitButton laws](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-cap-splitbutton-laws--docs)**
  — propagation across two actions, the joined seam, and what changes under RTL.
- **[CAP alternate appearance](?path=/docs/packages-visual-refresh-style-algebra-cap-alternate-appearance--docs)**
  — the cross-engine investigation, and how a law gets stated over an engine
  capability instead of an engine name.

## What was found

The classification table is the substance of the report. Three things are worth
saying about it up front.

**Two confirmed CAP defects, both invisible to review.** The dropped border
shorthands and the erased focus ring were both *legible* code: someone reading
the style hook would see a border colour and a focus reset and would be right
about what they were reading. They were wrong only about what the build and the
cascade would do with them. Neither is the kind of thing a careful reviewer
catches; both are the kind of thing a law catches immediately.

**Order dependence is a category, not an accident.** All Griffel classes carry
identical specificity, so when two declarations for the same property survive
`mergeClasses`, the winner is decided by stylesheet insertion order — which is
decided by what else the page rendered first. The ToggleButton substitution
policy defect had this shape: the same button computed `auto` alone and `none`
in company. A law asserted under a single render order would have reported
either result as correct. Laws in this suite that touch forced colors are
therefore asserted under more than one render order, each in its own page.

**The most dangerous findings were in the instrument.** Two of the entries below
are defects in the observer, not in CAP. Both made a law *pass* — one by folding
a `::after` divider into the element's own box, one by attributing LTR and RTL
rules to the same element. A law suite that reports failures honestly but passes
vacuously is worse than no law suite, because it converts silence into evidence.
They are published here at the same rank as the product defects.

## Why the audit cannot rot

Every correction in the table names an executable regression and a minimized
fixture, and a test asserts that pairing: a finding may not claim a correction
without citing a suite, the cited file must exist, and it must still contain the
cited suite name. Rename a describe block and the audit fails, rather than
quietly becoming a story about work that no longer holds.

## Where to look next

The nomination at the bottom of the page is measured, not intuited. Each column
of the census counts a marker for a mechanism this audit actually found —
unsupported shorthands the build will drop, focus overrides that can land in a
later bucket, forced-colors blocks that restate policy at equal specificity —
and asks where else in the theme the same thing could happen.

`react-tags` is nominated on both axes the issue asks for. On defect yield, it is
the only family in the theme still carrying raw unsupported border shorthands,
and it carries roughly twice the focus-override density of the family audited
here. On ownership overlap, `InteractionTag` composes a primary and a secondary
action into one control with a joined seam and shared disabled propagation:
structurally the same object as SplitButton. The laws already written transfer
almost unchanged, so what the next investigation costs is a fixture, not an
algebra.
